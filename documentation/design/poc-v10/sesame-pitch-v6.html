In the 2000s a restaurant needed a website. In the 2010s Google Maps and booking platforms took that role. Now the first visitor is often an AI: a guest asks an AI agent for a table and never opens a browser. That traffic still runs on HTTP and other classic protocols. By 2035 it will run on protocols specialized and optimized for agents. Don’t forget to say: human traffic on real websites won’t be fully replaced. It stays as the fallback. Small businesses already lost control once to the platforms. We do not want them to lose discovery a second time, and they should not need a developer to be ready.

BaselHack 2026·5

~35 s Ce que ça permet concrètement, c’est d’avoir un client qui recherche un restaurant avec une IA. Celle-ci va communiquer avec l’agent du restaurant cible et se chargera de la prise de RDV, etc. Le pro pourra ensuite, via son site de contrôle, parler à son agent pour tenir un état de ses réservations, affluences, etc. Why do this? So that a guest can search with their own AI agent, and a business can get an agent for its site. Sésame makes both possible. The guest just talks to an AI agent. It finds the restaurant and talks to the restaurant’s own agent, which answers from what the site says, offers times and prepares the booking. Nothing is booked until the guest says yes. The owner gives Sésame the website they already have and a few sentences about how they work. We read the site, build a profile, and generate a dedicated AI agent: it knows the menu and the practical details, and it calls its own booking tools, with its own tables, time slots and policies. Its model can’t write anything by itself: code runs a booking only on a confirmation. Day to day, the owner runs it by chatting: block a slot, change the max party size, see tonight’s bookings. No form anywhere, and the owner does not need a developer.
