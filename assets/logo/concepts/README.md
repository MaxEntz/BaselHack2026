# Concept review

All four concepts were drawn as SVG paths and rendered with librsvg at **16, 32 and 128 px**, on paper (`#FAF8F4`) and ink (`#182321`). The [contact sheet](contact-sheet.png) shows these raster samples at their native sizes. Each individual proof is in `renders/`.

| Concept | Visual assessment | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 — Open threshold | A doorway with a separate arriving element. The small element becomes a dot at 16 px, and the arch reads more like a magnet than a meeting. | Reject. |
| 02 — Counterpart | A projecting piece and a receiving piece form one compact silhouette. The stepped gap remains open at 16 px, and the relationship survives a single-color fill. | Select. |
| 03 — Rendezvous | Two hooked nodes suggest a connection. Their adjoining edges merge at 16 px; at larger sizes the silhouette evokes a generic chain-link icon. | Reject. |
| 04 — Common ground | Two shoulders form a shared passage. The overlap obscures the two-party idea, especially at 16 px, where it reads as a plain arch. | Reject. |

The selected mark was refined with 2-unit inner corner radii while retaining 12-unit outer radii and an 8-unit channel between its components on a 64-unit canvas. Its outer bounds are 8–56 on each axis. The two components remain separate without using contrasting colors, cutout overlays or masks. Final color proofs were inspected on both backgrounds; dark and light monochrome proofs were also inspected at all three sizes.

Similarity screening included web image searches for doorway and interlocking geometric marks, plus direct visual inspection of the [Huron mark](https://companieslogo.com/huron-consulting/logo/) returned by those searches. That example is a connected H; Counterpart uses two separated pieces and a stepped open channel. This limited comparison is not a reverse-image search and does not establish brand uniqueness. The downloaded reference image is not included in the deliverables.
